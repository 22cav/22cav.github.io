## The problem

A voice assistant built from a speech recognizer and an LLM usually works in
two steps. First it waits for you to finish talking and transcribes everything.
Then the LLM reads the whole transcript and starts to answer. The LLM sits idle
the whole time you are speaking.

## What I did

Rewind-Listen lets the LLM read the question while it is being spoken. At each
pause, or after 4 seconds of speech without one, a speech recognizer
transcribes the recent audio. The new text goes straight into the LLM's
key-value cache.

Recognizers often change their last few words when they hear more. When that
happens, the cache is rewound to the longest prefix the old and new transcripts
share, and only the changed part is read again. Because attention is causal, the
result is the same as reading the full transcript from scratch.

To decide when the question is finished, the system looks at two things: how
long the silence is, and how likely the LLM thinks the sentence is to end at
that point. The recognizer's final period or question mark is removed, so the
LLM has to predict it. The probability it gives to a sentence-ending next token
(`.`, `?`, `!` or its end-of-turn token) is $q_{\text{end}}$, and it comes free
with the forward pass that updates the cache. The turn ends after a silence of

$$
\tau_{\text{wait}} = 1.2\,\text{s} - 0.9\,\text{s} \times q_{\text{end}}
$$

So a sentence that looks complete ends after 0.3 s of silence, and one that
looks unfinished waits up to 1.2 s.

Nothing is trained, and the LLM is not modified. It uses an off-the-shelf
Parakeet recognizer (110M parameters) and any mlx-lm chat model, and it runs on
an Apple Silicon Mac with MLX.

## Results

Measured on an 8 GB MacBook Air M2 with Qwen3-1.7B (4-bit), on spoken
questions from VoiceBench:

- **Speed.** The first answer token comes 128 ms after the turn ends (median),
  against 513 ms when the recognizer and the LLM run one after the other: 4.0×
  sooner. LocalAgreement-2, a common streaming method that holds each word back
  until two recognizer runs agree on it, is about as fast but needs 2.2× the
  compute (5.6 s against 2.5 s per question).
- **Rewinds are cheap.** The median step rewinds no tokens at all. Over a whole
  question, the LLM reads 1.6× the length of the final transcript, against 6.8×
  if every step re-read it from scratch.
- **The result is exact.** In float32, the logits match reading the final
  transcript from scratch up to rounding, and 128-token answers are identical
  on all 40 test questions.
- **Ending the turn.** $q_{\text{end}}$ tells finished from unfinished sentences
  with an area under the ROC curve of 0.886, against 0.820 for the
  recognizer's own punctuation. With the rule above, the turn ends after a
  median wait of 600 ms. A fixed silence timer needs 960 ms to cut people off
  as rarely, so the wait is 38% shorter.
- **Accuracy.** No comparison shows a significant difference from the two-step
  pipeline: every 95% confidence interval includes zero.

| Task | Model | Two-step | Rewind-Listen | Difference [95% CI] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Multiple choice (1,064) | Qwen3-1.7B | 39.2% | 39.9% | +0.8 [−2.3, +3.8] |
| Big-Bench Hard (1,000) | Qwen3-1.7B | 54.6% | 54.0% | −0.6 [−4.1, +2.9] |
| Instruction following (345) | Qwen3-1.7B | 50.1% | 52.8% | +2.6 [−1.4, +6.7] |
| Multiple choice (300) | Qwen3-4B | 50.3% | 52.3% | +2.0 [−3.3, +7.7] |
| Multiple choice (300) | Llama-3.2-3B | 33.7% | 36.3% | +2.7 [−3.0, +8.3] |

The main limits: $q_{\text{end}}$ only reads words, so it cannot hear that a
speaker who pauses after a complete clause is going to go on. Only English was
tested, and most runs use synthetic speech, which is cleaner than live speech.
